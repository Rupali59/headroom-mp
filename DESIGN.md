## Problem Statement

For Claude Build Day, in a room of MP dignitaries whose brief is semiconductors,
defence and AI data centres, show MPSEDC and the MP AI department the state's grid as a
living thing: where it is weak, where it has failed before, where it is about to fail,
where an AI data centre could land, and what a named person should do about each. Every
judgement is flagged before it is made, not after. Operator, manager and owner see
different things. The UI stays simple because the room does not read software.

This is a **grid situational-awareness cockpit**, not a siting map. The existing
`mp-headroom-app.html` is roughly 20% of it, and the 20% it has (siting) is the part
Rupali explicitly deprioritised: *"ranking alone doesnt help, we will need a lot of
statistics to judge things and ranking 1 place or the other doesnt solve the problem."*

### What the existing artifacts are

| Artifact | What it is |
|---|---|
| `mp-headroom-brief.html` | Concept brief. Why (seasonal swing, night gap), what (3-step pipeline), for-MP, 9 out-of-frame constraints, 4 asks, roadmap. |
| `mp-headroom-app.html` | Single-file demo, 452 lines of which **157 are JS**. 19 hardcoded substations, day/night headroom **all mock**, only **4 of 19** carry a verified MVA (`mvaV:true`), distance-weighted ranking, templated answer typed out character by character. No LLM at runtime, no ingest pipeline, no write path. |
| `compass_artifact_*.md` | Research dossier. Source audit (14 sources), substation dataset with provenance, connectivity rules, night-power analysis, ingestion design, 1-hour build plan. |

The dossier's own caveats are the constraint that shapes this whole design:
MPPTCL per-substation margins are **robots.txt + Scribd blocked and not programmatically
retrievable**; **no published per-substation night coincident load exists**; MP SLDC
publishes real-time *state* load, not substation telemetry; NFMS feeder reliability is
not public.

## What Makes This Cool

**The room watches the data gap appear instead of reading about it.** Act 1 extracts a
real CTUIL PDF into cited records live, then hits the MPPTCL robots.txt wall on stage.
The four asks in the brief's §05 stop being a slide and become a thing that just
happened in front of the people who can fix it.

**Movement without a fake feed.** Act 2 toggles between two *real, cited* monthly
state-level peaks (Jan 2026 all-time peak 19,902 MW; Jul 2026 monsoon trough
13,818 MW) and models substation detail underneath, labelled. Movement, and nothing on
screen claims to be telemetry.

**One genuinely live number.** MP SLDC publishes real-time *state* load and the dossier's
source audit rates it Public / Real-time. A live state-load figure in the top bar,
labelled `Live · MP SLDC`, sits beside the replayed substation model and satisfies the
"near real-time" requirement with zero fakery. If it will not scrape reliably today, the
chrome says `Live state load: source available, not wired in this demo` — absence
attributable rather than silent.

**Nodes that refuse to answer.** A substation with two or more unknown inputs renders
hatched and is excluded from risk ranking, with the excluded count in the legend. In a
room of grid engineers, a tool that declines to guess is the credibility move.

**Twelve factors, twelve arguments, no score.** Act 3 gives a card per candidate with twelve
factors, each a verdict plus a one-line argument Claude wrote and a source. The roll-up
is a tally, never a composite number. **Three factors are *blocked* on three named
datasets** (1, 2, 3) and **two are `Unknown` as physical constraints** (7 water, 8 fibre).
Those are different things and the demo script must not conflate them.

## Constraints

- **Today.** Claude Build Day is 2026-09-20.
- **Must be deployed**, not opened from a laptop file path. See Stack and Deployment.
- **KISS UI.** *"the dept doesnt understand software engineering."* Three tabs, one role
  switch, one map. No nested menus. Each screen headed by its question in a sentence.
- **Audience can detect fakes.** MPSEDC/SLDC people know real telemetry. Simulation must
  be labelled in the chrome, not a footnote.
- **No real contact yet.** *"I am just building the demo, lets see how much confidence i
  get on the use case first."* The tool must work with zero cooperation from any agency.
- **Source-document quality is now measured, not assumed** (was unverified; probed
  2026-09-20, see Source Probe below).
- **The primary source document is marked RESTRICTED.** That is a decision to take
  deliberately, not by accident. See Source Probe.

## Premises

Stated in session, none challenged by Rupali.

1. **No public real-time feed exists for MP's grid at substation level.** Three
   independent statements in the dossier say so.
2. **A simulated live feed is the riskiest thing to put in front of this room.** A
   spotted fake tick discredits the honest work beside it. The inverse is the play:
   make the missing feed the product's own visible finding.
3. **The LLM does judgement; arithmetic stays deterministic.** Non-deterministic
   *scores* give two answers on two runs in front of engineers. Claude writes the
   argument per factor; the roll-up is plain arithmetic.
4. **"Flag before judgement" is a data-model rule, not a UI rule.** Confidence and
   provenance attach to a field before it feeds a score; a score refuses to render below
   threshold. "We do not know, and here is what we would need" is a first-class output.
5. **Operator, manager and owner are three questions, not three permission levels.**
   Operator: what is wrong now, what do I do in the next hour. Manager: where do we
   invest, what do I tell the minister. Owner: who signed off on this number.

## Approaches Considered

Three were offered. Rupali chose **all three, in different areas** — which is a better
answer than the menu, because sequencing them as three acts over one data model resolves
the credibility problem that made any single one of them weak alone.

- **A — Operations + Opportunity cockpit.** Roles, write path, 10-factor rubric, no fake
  feed. Strongest on truth, weakest on opening impact. → becomes Acts 2 and 3.
- **B — Fault-prevention control room.** Simulated telemetry, alarm states, pre-fault
  worklist. Closest to the literal description; highest credibility risk. → its honest
  half (risk view, worklist, history) becomes Act 2; its simulated telemetry is replaced
  by dated replay.
- **C — Evidence engine.** Claude reads government PDFs on stage. Highest audience wow,
  delivers none of the operations asks. → becomes Act 1, the opener.

### Rejected during the session

- **Ranking substations 1..N.** Rejected by Rupali directly: ranking does not solve the
  problem. Act 3 tallies verdicts per factor instead of ordering candidates.
- **Live telemetry, real or simulated.** Rejected under premises 1 and 2. Replaced by
  dated replay over two real load days.
- **A single composite LLM score across 10 factors.** Rejected under premise 3.
  Non-determinism lives in the prose, never in the number.

## Recommended Approach

**Three acts, one record, three roles.**

### The spine: every fact is a Field, not a value

The current app stores `day:180, night:90`. Every downstream problem follows from that.
Replace each fact with:

```js
{ v: 1630, unit: "MVA",
  conf: "verified" | "derived" | "modelled" | "field" | "unknown",
  src: "tnd85", page: null, asOf: "2024-06",
  by: "ingest" | "<person, org>", at: <timestamp>, note: "" }
```

Three things fall out for free, and they are the three things Rupali asked for:

| Falls out | Answers |
|---|---|
| `conf:"unknown"` renders hatched, never coloured; excluded from ranking with a visible count | "flag before judgement", premise 4 |
| An operator correction is a new Field with `conf:"field"`, which outranks `modelled` | "no user role which can help input things" |
| The Owner view is a table of `by` / `at` / `src` | "manager and owner would have different responsibilities", Q7 |

Build this first. Everything else consumes it.

### Act 1 — Ingest. "Where this comes from."

- **Two** real documents preloaded and genuinely extractable: CTUIL bulk-consumer list,
  CTUIL Annex-C margins. They live server-side in the repo; the browser POSTs a document
  **id**, never bytes (Vercel caps request bodies near 4.5 MB, far below Anthropic's
  32 MB, and a stage upload is a failure mode with no upside).
- **Extract** streams a messy multi-row table into cited records in front of the room,
  via `POST /api/extract`. With a key present this is a real call; without one the
  button reads **"Replay recorded extraction"** and is labelled REPLAY. It never says
  "Extract" while replaying.
- **Page numbers come from the API, not from us.** Setting `citations: {enabled: true}`
  on the PDF document block makes Claude return `page_location` with
  `start_page_number` / `end_page_number` per cited span. The brief's promise — *"Every
  field carries document URL + page + date"* — is a platform feature, not something to
  hand-build. This is the single biggest saving in the build.
- The MPPTCL STU margin list appears as a **recorded artifact**, labelled RECORDED with a
  timestamp: the `curl -A` transcript and the `robots.txt` text. It is **not** a live
  fetch. An earlier draft had it both preloaded and failing, which cannot both be true —
  and a live third-party fetch to `mptransco.in` in the opening 90 seconds is a
  non-deterministic dependency that may simply not fail. Say it precisely, because the
  dossier's own caveat is that *"browser download bypasses robots.txt"*: this is an
  **automation** barrier, not an access barrier. An engineer in the room knows that.
- Both feed a **Blocked sources** panel. That panel is the brief's §05 ask, generated by
  the product rather than asserted by a slide.
- **Act 1's output does not feed Acts 2 and 3.** They read the committed dataset. This is
  stated so the coupling is not accidental: if a live extraction fails on stage, the rest
  of the demo is unaffected.

### Act 2 — Operations. "What is happening."

- Map coloured by **risk**, not headroom.
- **Two labelled buttons, not a scrubber.** `Winter peak · Jan 2026 · 19,902 MW (CEA via
  CEIC)` and `Monsoon trough · Jul 2026 · 13,818 MW`. **The sources are monthly. There is
  no day and no clock time** — an earlier draft of this document invented `15 Jan 2026
  19:30`, which appears in none of the three source artifacts, and put it next to a
  citation. A two-stop slider pretending to be continuous is the same failure in a
  different costume. Substation detail beneath is `modelled` and says so.
- **Risk is five factors and one line of arithmetic, both shown.** Loading ratio,
  night-supply dependence (thermal share), seasonal coincidence with the irrigation peak,
  planned outage or under-construction, single-transformer exposure. Each scores
  Strong / Adequate / Weak / Unknown.

  ```
  risk = count(factors scoring Weak)   // of 5
  0 -> green   1-2 -> amber   3+ -> red
  >= 2 of the 5 Unknown -> hatched, excluded from risk, counted in the legend
  ```

  The hatching rule is scoped to **these five risk factors**, everywhere it is mentioned.
  Premise 3 says the arithmetic is deterministic and visible; this is that arithmetic, and
  it belongs on screen next to the legend.

- **Preventive.** Each at-risk node gets one sentence in a fixed form: *"[condition] +
  [no mitigation before date] -> [action], owner [role]."* Worked example from material
  already in the dossier: *"single 400 kV transformer, no augmentation before Kurawar
  ~Jun 2027 -> schedule a second ICT bay now, owner MPPTCL planning."* This is the
  "steps before grid faults might occur" requirement, and it costs one line of UI.
- **Rectification worklist** — the operator's screen. Each row: the flag, the evidence,
  a suggested action, a status (Open → Acknowledged → Acted → Closed), an assignee.
  Operator can add a flag, correct a field, or close with a note. Manager sees the same
  list read-only with aggregate counts.
- **History, split at today.** Two visually distinct halves, because a strip that merges
  them is the same class of error as an unlabelled mock — and the dossier says so
  explicitly: *"treat forward-looking SCODs as scheduled, not delivered."*
  - **Delivered:** Mandsaur 500 MVA transformer (2024); Manglia +200 MVA (Apr 2026);
    MPPMCL BESS bids closed (30 Jul 2026).
  - **Scheduled:** Kurawar 765 kV (~Jun 2027); Morena solar-plus-storage (Dec 2027);
    Gadarwara-II Part B (to Mar 2031).

  The gap between the two halves *is* the preventive lead time above.

**D8 — the band is the anchor, and cards are gone everywhere else.** Hard rejection #7
(*app UI made of stacked cards instead of layout*) was a genuine hit, and D3 caused it:
shrinking the map removed the only dominant element, and the findings rail added a third
column of boxes beside a worklist already carrying three card treatments. The fix is a
full-width findings band using **rules and typography, not boxes**, which restores a
reading order — the finding, then the geography, then what to do — matching how an operator
actually thinks. **Card chrome survives in exactly one place: a worklist row, because
acting on it is the interaction.**

Rejected: returning the map to half-canvas with leader-line callouts (strongest anchor and
best use of geography, but colliding callouts on clustered nodes — Neemuch and Mandsaur
already overlap — and the most build time on the day with least); keeping four columns and
only unifying card style (cheapest, but litmus 2 stays NO and it still reads as a flat
admin panel).

**D2 — the top bar carries one time claim.** The live MP SLDC figure moves OUT of the top
bar into the context rail above the load-condition buttons, where it reads as context for
the choice rather than a competing clock. The bar states the REPLAY condition only. **The
role switch moves left of the tabs at full contrast**, because it scopes them and changes
the meaning of every number below; it was the smallest, faintest control and the most
consequential.

Rejected: one combined status sentence (slower under stage lighting, costs a band);
dropping the live figure (the only genuinely real-time number, and it answers the stated
near-real-time requirement).

**D3 — findings in words, map as locator.** Reasoning in text survives a projector and a
back row; small coloured dots do not. Every finding clicks through to provenance, which is
how the two-click success criterion is met.

**Litmus after D8:** brand YES · anchor **YES (the band)** · scannable YES · one job per
section YES · cards necessary **YES (one use, and it is the actionable one)** · motion
improves hierarchy YES (Act 1's streaming carries information) · premium without shadows
YES. Hard rejections: **0 of 7**.

**Consequence to carry into Pass 6:** three columns plus a band is a 1280px-minimum
layout. The collapse order below that is a responsive decision, not a detail.

### Confidence ladder and the coincidence model — decided in design review (D5)

**The wireframe showed 2 hatched nodes. Applied literally, the rule gives 15.** Only 4 of
19 records carry a verified MVA, and headroom cannot be modelled without a capacity. That
inconsistency is resolved here rather than at build time, with a three-rung ladder that
matches the data actually held:

| Rung | Which nodes | Capacity from | Headroom | Renders |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `verified` | 4 — Bhopal BDTCL, Suky Sewaniya, Bina, Mandsaur | cited document | modelled | full verdict, solid node |
| `derived` | 13 — known voltage class, no published MVA | **voltage-class typical**: 765 kV ≈ 2×1500 MVA, 400 kV ≈ 1000 MVA, 220 kV ≈ 300 MVA | modelled, low confidence | verdict shown, node carries a dashed outline |
| `unknown` | Kurawar (under construction) and any node failing both | — | none | hatched, excluded, counted in legend |

**Nothing is labelled `mock`, because nothing is invented.** A stated model is not a guess;
that distinction is the whole difference between this and the existing demo.

**The model is DELETED. 2026-09-20 — it was structurally degenerate.**

As specified it collapsed algebraically:

```
headroom_i = m*0.9*0.85 - P*(m/N) = m*(0.765 - P/N)
```

Headroom was a **fixed multiple of capacity**, so risk became a pure restatement of
substation size — night, season and location never entered it. Both denominators give a
degenerate map: summing over our 19 nodes makes all 18 negative (all red), summing over
the 85,284 MVA network makes all 18 positive (all green). No colour function built on it
could discriminate anything.

**What replaces it: the source document already publishes the answer.** CTUIL's
Annex-IV(3) carries solar-hour and non-solar-hour access and margins **per substation** —
which is precisely the day/night distinction this product exists to answer. Mandsaur PS,
row 53 of the fixture: solar-hour access 250 MW, non-solar 200 MW, margins 50/100 per bay,
0/50 at the station, 4 and 2 spare bays.

So night headroom is `verified`, cited to a page, for every substation in the document,
and `unknown` for every substation outside it. The hatched set becomes **principled**
rather than an artefact of which substations happened to get a press release.

**The caveat stays on the card, because it is real:** these margins are
*generation-injection* framed. They describe how much renewable power a substation can
export, which is a useful proxy for how much a data centre can draw, not the same thing.
A published proxy with a citation beats a model with no discriminating power.

### Operator journey and the gap moment — decided in design review (D6, D7)

The 8-minute script storyboards the **presenter**. This storyboards the **operator**, who
is who the product is for.

| # | Operator does | Feels | Design answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opens Act 2, winter peak | *Is this current?* | One time claim in the bar (D2) |
| 2 | Scans the findings rail | *Three problems, named* | Findings in words (D3) |
| 3 | Hits a node nobody can assess | **Is this broken, or is the data?** | **D6 — see below** |
| 4 | Clicks a red node | *Why red?* | Risk formula + the five factors |
| 5 | Disagrees | **Frustration, then agency** | The write path, one click from the verdict |
| 6 | Corrects a value | *Did that count?* | The hatch visibly resolves (D4) |
| 7 | Returns tomorrow | *Is it still here?* | **D7 — it is not, and the UI now says so** |

**D6 — every gap names its holder.** A hatch that says only "cannot assess" is read three
ways: your tool is broken, the public data is thin, or *my organisation holds that number*.
Only the third produces a pilot, and it is the least likely default when a stranger's tool
shows you a blank. So each gap states absence, holder and consequence in one line:

> *Transformation capacity not published for Katni. **MPPTCL** holds this figure. With it,
> Katni joins 17 assessable substations.*

The Blocked sources panel aggregates these, so brief §05 is **generated from the data**
rather than asserted on a slide. Step 3 stops being the weakest moment and becomes the
one that converts.

**Copy constraint, and it is not optional:** naming an agency beside a gap must carry zero
accusation, in Hindi and English both. State what is not published and what it would
unlock. Never why it is missing. In a government room the difference between those two
sentences is the difference between a pilot and a closed door.

**D7 — the UI states what it can keep.** Corrections are `localStorage`. The provenance
row therefore reads `by R. Sharma, MPPTCL Bhopal · 20 Sep 14:31 · this session`, and the
ledger header reads *"Corrections are held in this browser for the demo. A pilot writes
them to the shared record."* One phrase, and the interface stops claiming more than it
delivers in the component whose entire job is making provenance trustworthy. It doubles as
the pilot pitch, delivered exactly where someone is feeling the value.

Ceiling recorded: shared persistence (Vercel KV / Upstash) is the upgrade. Trigger: the
first time two people need to see each other's corrections.

made visible.

### Roles

One switch in the top bar.

- **Operator** — can write (flags, corrections, worklist status). Lands on Act 2.
- **Manager** — read-only on facts, sees aggregates, can export a one-page brief. Lands
  on Act 3.
- **Owner** — **lands on Act 2**, ledger view. The provenance ledger is a drawer inside
  Act 2, not a fourth surface: every field, who set it, when, from what.

### Demo script, 8 minutes

1. **Act 1, 90s.** Drop the CTUIL PDF, watch it become cited records. Then MPPTCL fails.
   "That wall is the ask."
2. **Act 2, 3 min.** Operator role. Replay the 15 Jan winter night peak; three nodes go
   red, two go hatched. Open the worklist, flag one, correct a value, watch provenance
   update. Switch to Manager for the aggregate.
3. **Act 3, 2.5 min.** Manager role. 100 MW, 24x7. Twelve factors, three blocked.
   "Factors 1, 2 and 3 are blocked on exactly three datasets: MPPTCL margins, SLDC load
   profiles, MPPMCL storage milestones. That is the ask." (Water and fibre are *physical*
   constraints from brief §04 item 08, not data asks — do not conflate them. The MPSEDC
   pilot-partner ask is a person, not a dataset, and belongs in the closing.)
4. **Owner, 60s.** The ledger. "Every number on that screen has a name and a date."

## Source Probe — measured 2026-09-20, before any build

`extraction-test/` holds the harness and the fixtures. Findings so far, all from the real
document rather than the dossier's description of it:

| Question | Measured |
|---|---|
| Does the CTUIL margins PDF fetch? | **Yes.** Plain `curl`, HTTP 200, 1,443,434 bytes, `application/pdf`. **No bot-wall on this document** — the blocked one is the *MPPTCL STU* list, a different file. Act 1's "two documents that genuinely extract" is confirmed available |
| Is there a text layer, or is it scans? | **Text layer.** 29 pages, 293,218 extractable characters. Not an OCR problem |
| Does it contain Madhya Pradesh at all? | **Yes.** Western Region begins p17. Mandsaur PS, Neemuch PS, Rajgarh SS (Sec-I and Sec-II), Indore (PG), plus Rewa Ultra Mega Solar's Agar, Shajapur and Neemuch solar-park entries |
| Is the table actually messy enough to need an LLM? | **Yes, and this is the strongest single argument for Act 1.** 16 columns, a 9-line multi-row header, and **merged Substation cells**: rows for the Agar and Shajapur solar parks have an *empty* substation cell inheriting from a cell above. A naive parser carries the last seen label forward and gets them wrong |
| Size against the caps | 1.44 MB full, 610 KB for the 4-page MP slice. Under Anthropic's 32 MB *and* under Vercel's ~4.5 MB body cap — so a browser upload would technically work. Keep it server-side anyway; a stage upload has no upside |

### Two findings that change decisions

**1. The document is marked RESTRICTED, in both languages.** Every page carries

### Three Next.js traps this design walks into

Each fails in a way that does not look like its cause.

1. **Serverless timeout — and streaming does not save you.** Streaming defeats the
   *gateway idle* timeout; the function is still killed at `maxDuration`, which is
   plan-capped. An earlier draft said streaming "avoids an HTTP timeout," which is wrong
   in the way that kills a demo mid-sentence. Set `export const maxDuration` explicitly in
   both route handlers and **run the worst-case Act 3 call end to end before rehearsal**,
   not during it.
2. **localStorage during render is a hydration mismatch.** The server has no `window`.
   Read persisted worklist state in a `useEffect` after mount and seed from a known
   empty default, never during render or in a `useState` initialiser.
3. **Tailwind v4 has no JS config.** Palette, fonts and spacing go in `globals.css`
   under `@theme`. A `tailwind.config.js` copied from a v3 project is ignored silently,
   and the symptom is "my colours did nothing".

### Streaming from a route handler

**`client.beta.messages.stream()`**, not `client.messages.stream()` — fast mode requires
the beta namespace, and both beta flags go in one array:
`betas: ["fast-mode-2026-02-01", "server-side-fallback-2026-07-01"]`.

It yields an async iterable. Wrap it in a `ReadableStream`, enqueue newline-delimited
JSON, return it as the `Response` body; the client reads with
`response.body.getReader()`. Emit one record per line as it completes, so Act 1's table
fills in front of the room rather than appearing all at once. The streaming *is* the demo
moment.

**Fast mode is a research preview with its own separate rate limit.** A 429 mid-demo is a
live risk: catch it, drop `speed`, retry at standard rate. Note that switching speed
invalidates the prompt cache.

### Claude API specifics

Verified against the bundled `claude-api` skill, not recalled.

- **Model: `claude-opus-5`** on both routes. $5 / $25 per MTok, 1M context. Extraction
  from messy multi-row tables is judgement work, and the ten factor arguments are what
  the room will read closely.
- **Fast mode is NOT available and the recommendation is withdrawn.** Measured
  2026-09-20 on this organisation: `rate limit of 0 fast mode input tokens per minute
  (model: claude-opus-5)`. A ceiling of **zero** is a provisioning fact wearing a
  rate-limit costume — the documented "retry after the delay" mitigation loops forever.
  `extract.ts` now treats `--fast` as opt-in and exits 2 with that diagnosis. Moot for
  the demo regardless: ingest runs locally, ahead of time.
- **Thinking:** `{type: "adaptive", display: "summarized"}`. `budget_tokens` is a 400 on
  Opus 5. `display` defaults to omitted, which reads on stage as a long silent pause, so
  set it explicitly.
- **PDF input:** base64 document block, no beta header, no Files API upload step.
  `{type:"document", source:{type:"base64", media_type:"application/pdf", data:<b64>},
  title, citations:{enabled:true}}`. Limits 32 MB per request, 600 pages. The base64
  string must carry no newlines.
- **Page numbers come from the API.** `citations:{enabled:true}` returns `page_location`
  with 1-indexed `start_page_number` / `end_page_number` per cited span. Largest single
  saving in the build — but it delivers **page, not URL or date**. The response carries
  `document_index` and `document_title` only, so the URL and as-of mapping stays yours.
  The brief's *"document URL + page + date"* is two-thirds yours and one-third free.
- **Citations are all-or-none** across document blocks in a request. If Act 1 sends both
  CTUIL documents, both need `citations:{enabled:true}`.
- **The non-obvious constraint: citations and structured outputs are mutually
  exclusive.** `citations:{enabled:true}` with `output_config.format` returns a 400.
  Since citations are the whole point of Act 1, get schema-valid JSON the other way: a
  tool definition with top-level `strict: true` and `additionalProperties: false`.
- **Refusal fallbacks:** include `betas:["server-side-fallback-2026-07-01"]` with
  `fallbacks: "default"`, and check `stop_reason === "refusal"` before reading
  `content`.
- **Cost:** a few PDF pages plus ten short arguments per candidate. Cents, even at
  fast-mode rates. Not a constraint.
