Headroom MP  ·  Claude × Madhya Pradesh AI Seminar  ·  Concept brief

Every AI ambition in MP runs on one question: where's the power at 2 a.m.?

Madhya Pradesh wants AI data centres. They need 50–100 MW of power, around the clock, from a grid built around winter irrigation and midday solar. Headroom MP maps where that load can connect, and whether it will stay on after sunset, using the government's own documents.

The honest problem

MP is pushing solar hard, with a stated goal of meeting half its power needs from solar by 2030. But solar produces nothing at night, and a GPU cluster does not sleep. Night supply today comes from roughly 15 GW of thermal plants, while the first large battery tenders (625 MW / 1,250 MWh at Bina, Sendhwa and Birsinghpur) only closed bids in July 2026.

Meanwhile, an investor asking "can I put 100 MW near Bhopal?" has to read transmission PDFs, regulator orders and tender notices by hand. No single view answers it.

04 — Out of frame

The real problems the demo doesn't solve.

A demo that hides these would be a nicer slide and a worse product. These are the constraints anyone building AI infrastructure in MP will hit.

Data gapPhysical constraintProcess / policy
01

No load-side ISTS node exists in MP

CTUIL's monthly list of inter-state substations for bulk consumers covers Odisha, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh. Madhya Pradesh has none, so a 50–100 MW data centre realistically connects through the state network at 220/400 kV.

Physical
02

Intra-state margins aren't machine-readable

MPPTCL's list of EHV substations with available margin (MVA) is an undated PDF behind robots.txt and third-party mirrors. The single most useful dataset for this question can't be ingested automatically today.

Data gap
03

Published margins describe generation, not load

CTUIL's solar/non-solar margins tell you how much renewable power a substation can export. They are a useful proxy, but not the same as how much a data centre can safely draw.

Data gap
04

Nobody publishes night load per substation

Night headroom has to be modelled from transformation capacity and state-level load curves. Without substation-level coincident load, every night figure is an estimate.

Data gap
05

Night power is still thermal

Storage is coming but not here: MPPMCL's 625 MW / 1,250 MWh BESS bids were due July 2026, and Morena's solar-plus-storage is scheduled for December 2027. Until then, 24×7 AI load leans on coal.

Physical
06

Transmission arrives on a multi-year clock

The Kurawar 765 kV substation is expected around June 2027; parts of the Gadarwara-II / Mandsaur augmentation run to March 2031. Data centres are built in 18 months.

Physical
07

Connection rules add friction

Bulk consumers of 50 MW+ can apply to CTU for ISTS connectivity, but the GNA regulations don't allow dual (part inter-state, part intra-state) connectivity, and each route has its own fees and approvals.

Policy
08

Power is only one of the inputs

Cooling water, fibre routes, land and skilled staff all decide real sites. None of them are in this demo.

Physical
09

The citizen side stays invisible

Feeder-level reliability for homes and farms sits in the National Feeder Monitoring System, which isn't public. A grid tool for investors shouldn't be the only grid tool that exists.

Data gap
05 — The ask

Four datasets turn a demo into a tool.

MPPTCL / STU

Substation margins as CSV or API

Dated, per-substation available MVA and bay availability, published on a fixed cadence.

MP SLDC

Substation-level load profiles

Hourly or 15-minute loading for 220 kV and above, even anonymised or lagged by a quarter.

MPPMCL

Storage & RTC pipeline status

BESS and round-the-clock procurement milestones by node, so night headroom can be projected.

MPSEDC

A pilot partner

One real investor enquiry run through the tool end to end, with feedback from the people who answer those enquiries today.
